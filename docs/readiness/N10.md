# N10 — Outcome constraints (M5, part 2)

State: implemented and tested (PR #96, 2026-10-28); the acceptance test below is Hoseung's.

## What was wrong

Layer: evidence. A condition on a measured result (a floor on a second metric, a cap on a measured cost) had no form at
all: it became a recorded metric plus words. Nothing kept a result that broke it from being a run's best, so a method
that reached a low thermal resistance by blowing past the fan's pressure limit ranked first in the comparison and the
ledger.
Invariant: INV-N10-1, INV-N10-2 (added, in `docs/invariants/N10.md`).

## What changed

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| A charter constraint of form `outcome`: a true/false `test` over the objective and the metrics the evaluator reports, by name, and number quantities. The `qualitative` form is described as policy rules no code can judge | `contracts/problem_charter.py` `Constraint` |
| `OutcomeConstraint` (an expression, its constants at the instance); a problem instance carries them in `outcomes`, left out of the dump while empty so older records keep their digests and bytes. `outcome_checks()` says, per constraint, whether a result meets it; a metric the evaluator did not report breaks it. `same_evaluation()` compares instances without them, so an installed adapter still resolves an instance the charter added them to | `contracts/problems.py` `OutcomeConstraint`, `ProblemInstance` |
| A declared problem's manifest carries them (and refuses one that reads a name it does not report); an installed problem's setup and task carry them to the task's problem | `contracts/evaluators.py` `EvaluatorManifest`; `contracts/problem_imports.py` `ProblemSetup`; `contracts/requests.py` `TaskInput` |
| Findings: an outcome constraint that reads a name that is not the objective, a metric or a number quantity, reads no objective or metric, or gives no single true or false is blocking; on an installed problem, one that reads a metric the problem does not report is blocking (every result would break it) | `problem_charter/charter.py` `outcome_findings()`, `evaluator_findings()` |
| `manifest()` and `compile_setups()` put them on every instance with its thresholds | `problem_charter/charter.py` `outcome_constraints()` |

An observation with a false constraint stays in the journal but out of the archive: `best_objective` is the best feasible result. Progress counts `infeasible_observations` | `execution/worker.py` |
| The general report compares runs held to other outcome constraints apart, and each run, method summary and group says how many results were infeasible. | The Charter page names the form ("a condition on the measured result, checked on every result"); the Experiments page and the Evolution frontier show "N infeasible" under a run or strategy that had some | `frontend/src/note/CharterPanel.tsx`, `experiments/ExperimentsPage.tsx`, `evolution/EvolutionPage.tsx` |

## Checked

- `tests/balsamic/test_outcome_constraints.py` (7 tests): a cooler charter's pressure cap reaches the declared problem
  with each instance's threshold, and its instance says which results break it (including one that does not report the
  metric); the findings on what an outcome expression reads; a manifest refuses one on an unreported metric; an
  installed problem's tasks carry the charter's outcome constraints, and one on a metric the problem does not report is
  blocking; records without them keep their bytes and the evaluation identity does not change; a run whose best raw
  score breaks the floor reports the best feasible one, and its journal keeps the infeasible results marked; the
  comparison ranks the method with feasible results first and counts the infeasible ones, and so does the ledger.
- `frontend/tests/charter.spec.ts` "an outcome constraint reads as a condition on the measured result" and
  `frontend/tests/evolution.spec.ts` "the frontier says how many of a strategy's results broke a rule on the outcome"
  (Playwright).
- The full suite (counts in the PR).

## Not done

- A method is told a result was infeasible only through the observation's `constraints`. A function method gets the
  raw score from `evaluate()` and may steer towards infeasible results; the run's best is still the feasible one.
  Built-in methods do not use the flag either.
- An outcome expression reads single numbers, not the charter's tables.
- The MEENT-specific comparison (`analysis/comparison.py`, `best_efficiency`) is unchanged: its problems declare no
  outcome constraints.
- A soft outcome constraint is treated as hard: no penalty form exists.

## Acceptance test

1. Start the app, create a campaign and describe a problem with a limit on a measured result, for example: "a heat sink
   on a 50 mm base with 2 to 12 fins, each 0.5 to 5 mm wide and 5 to 50 mm tall; minimise the thermal resistance; the
   fan gives at most 40 Pa, so the pressure drop across the fins must stay within that; report the pressure drop".
2. Open the Charter page. The pressure drop is a recorded metric, and under "Constraints" the fan rule reads
   "outcome: a condition on the measured result, checked on every result; one that breaks it never counts as the best",
   with its condition (pressure drop at most 40 Pa). It is not a "rule in words only" check.
3. Change the rule to read a metric the problem does not report: a blocking check says so. Put it back.
4. Confirm the charter and run "Uniform random" once its evaluator exists. On the Experiments page, a run that tried
   designs over the fan's limit shows "N infeasible" under its evaluations, and its Best is the best design within the
   limit. On the Evolution page's frontier the same count shows under the strategy's best score, and the comparison ranks only
   results within the limit.
